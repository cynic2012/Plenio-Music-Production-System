# Apply Arrangement

> **Experimental.** Creative modes and the closeness sliders can give unexpected results - a chord that surprises, an instrument line that does not fit every song, a plan that is not applied. They are meant for experimenting first: try a mode, listen, change the score in the Song Sheet or run again with another *arrangement seed*, and keep what you like. *off* is the dependable choice.

Writes the writer's section plan into the score - or keeps the score as it was and says why. Plenio writes every note itself, with the same validated operations as *Score Tools*, so the result is always a score that YuE2 and the score editor read.

Per section, in this order (the *harmony guard*):

1. **chords** - the plan's chords, one per bar. A chord stands where it belongs to the section's key (detected from its notes when the written key is clearly wrong) or to the genre's usual borrowings (the brief's genre and the style choose the family: pop, rock, EDM, folk, soul, jazz, blues, metal, cinematic, hip hop, latin; a secondary dominant only when it resolves) and carries the melody of its whole bar - no note that grinds against it on a strong beat or held for a beat (a half step above a chord tone, a half step under its major seventh, the #4 beside its fifth, a blue minor third over a major triad). Where it does not, a small dynamic programme over the section chooses the nearest chord that does (the original, the same root in another colour, an in-key chord sharing two tones) and the report says why. A score without chords gets none (YuE2 harmonises it). In a cover below song flow closeness 80 the old melody weighs only as much as the closeness says, so the writer's chords win more often the lower it is.
2. **the melody** (a cover below song flow closeness 80, sung or played by the instrument line) - fitted to the new chords: from 50 a note that clashes moves to the nearest chord tone (by a step when it is short and off the beat); below 50 the melody is written anew - chord tones on the accents, small steps, phrases ending on a chord tone, near the old line at 49 and freer down to 0. A sung melody keeps its rhythm (its syllables stay on their notes); an instrument line that carries the melody may get new rhythms below 45. The report says how many notes moved (*melody follows the chords (9 note(s))*).
3. **the instrument line** (the score's *Ins* voice) - what the plan's *lead* asks for: *pad*, *arpeggio*, *riff*, *countermelody*, *solo*, *octave* (the sung melody an octave away; only with lines under the singing), *motif* (the writer's short figure in note names, moved onto every chord along the key's scale, restarted on every downbeat, its strong-beat notes on chord notes and no clash on any beat), *none* or *keep*. Every note follows the chord sounding at it. By default the line plays only **where the voice rests** (fills and answers - YuE2's own scores never sound *Ins* under the singing); with the brief's *lines under the singing* it also sounds under the voice, calm, below it and never a minor second or major seventh against it. A section sung through has no room for a fill (reported). A line that carries the melody (an instrumental with a lead) is never replaced.
4. **gate** - a section that clashes more than before (melody against chords - accented, or a larger share of its time -, voice against line in the whole song) goes back to how it was.
5. **key lift** (only the mode *key change* plans one) - the section moves by the planned semitones, unless the voice would leave its range (at most two semitones above the song's highest note). A lift **holds to the end of the song** (a later section the plan puts back keeps it), and is **led in by chords**, as a modulation is in the textbooks: a chord of both keys (the new key's ii or IV, iv or VI in minor) and then the new key's dominant (*Cm - D7 -> Gm*), or the new key's lowered sixth and seventh steps rising to it (*Eb - F -> Gm*, *Bb - C -> D*) - the most room first - a bar each over the last two bars, then over the last one and a half, then half a bar each in the last one; the dominant alone, or a single leading chord, only where the melody leaves no room for more. Every chord must clash with nothing sung under it, and Plenio's own instrument line in these bars moves onto the leading chords' tones (it rests where none fits the voice; an instrumental's lead line stays and must fit as it is). A lifted section that starts before its first chord is not led in (a leading chord would sound on into it). The report names the sequence (*bars 42-43: Eb - F lead into Gm*). A lift that one of its sections cannot take stays out in all of them. Both from a listening test (study E7): the lifts were good, but sudden, and a last chorus lifted with the outro back down was the most abrupt moment.

At the end the **tempo** change. Then the result must pass YuE2's parser, read back unchanged, open note by note in the score editor and - with the engine connected - fit YuE2's context (the instrument lines are left out first; a cover's style and lyrics are estimated, as they are written later). Anything else keeps the score as it was: status *fallback* with the reason.

- **answer** - the writer's answer; requested only when a plan is wanted (not with arrangement *off*, not for a cover at song flow closeness 95 or more, not without a score). Read leniently: code fences, thoughts, a missing last brace, single quotes, chord aliases (*Cmaj9* -> *Cmaj7*, *H* -> *B*) are repaired and reported.
- **seed** - varies the notes Plenio writes for the lines (the same seed, the same notes).
- **engine**, **style**, **lyrics** (optional) - for the exact context budget.
- **first** (optional) - the first Apply Arrangement's **state**: this node is then the **re-ask round** (below).

**The re-ask (once).** Where the harmony guard had to repair more than a third of a section's bars of the writer's chords, or a section went back through the gate, the node writes a **reask_prompt** (with its **reask_schema**): those sections only, with what did not fit (*bar 1: Db is not a chord of C in pop*), the melody's notes of every bar and the chords that fit their key. In the *Arrange* block a second writer answers it - only then - and the second Apply Arrangement (its **first** input linked to the first one's **state**) arranges the plan again with these sections replaced. The second arrangement is used when it needs fewer repairs and clashes no more; otherwise the first stands. The report says which (*asked again about section(s) 1: the second plan needs fewer repairs (4 -> 0) and is used*). Never more than once.

Outputs: the **score**, **reask_prompt**, **reask_schema**, **state** (for the re-ask round) and a **report** with the status (*applied*, *partial* - some parts kept, see the notes -, *unchanged*, *fallback*, *skipped*), the idea, the **harmony check** before and after (melody on chord tones, accented clashes with a chord, clashes between voice and line, chords in the key), per section what was applied and what stayed, and the plan itself. Connect the report to the Song Sheet's **arrangement** input: the sheet shows it and warns about a fallback, and the release record keeps it.
