# Cover Brief

The intent of a cover. Melody, song form and tempo come from the source recording; the brief says what the new version sounds like and what happens to the vocals and the harmony.

- **mode**
  - *one cover, stop to review* (default) - Song Sheets set to *as the brief says* stop for review (the score, then the lyrics); after approval every run is a new take.
  - *new cover every run* - no stops; every run writes a new version (title, style and - with *new lyrics* - the lyrics) and renders it. The transcription of the source is reused. The batch count next to **Run** makes a series.
- **template** - an optional target style. Style fields you leave empty are taken from the template (never the language).
- **description, genre, mood** - the target style. At least a genre or a description is needed.
- **vocals**
  - *instrumental* (default) - an instrument plays the vocal melody (*instrument plays the lead*, optional **lead instrument**) or *accompaniment only*.
  - *original lyrics* - the source's lyrics, transcribed by *Transcribe Lyrics*. **language** *auto* detects it; **voice** describes the new singer.
  - *new lyrics* - new words on the source's melody: **language**, **voice**, **theme**; **phrasing reference** lets the writer see the source's transcribed lines (runs the lyrics ASR) and gives it a syllable target per line.
- **harmony** - *new accompaniment* removes the source's chords so YuE2 re-harmonises (melody mode); *keep original chords* keeps them (full mode).
- **title** (advanced) - a fixed title. Empty: the cover is named after its source recording - the recording's title tag, else its file name, with `-cover` (`Morning Song-cover`), so a cover is easy to match with its source. That needs the **source** input: the source's *Load Audio* node, connected in the *2 · YuE2 · Cover* template. Without it the writer proposes a title.
- **arrangement (experimental)** - the creative mode (creative modes can give unexpected results: try them, listen, keep what you like). *off* (default): the transcribed score stays as it is. A creative mode lets the writer model re-arrange the score's sections (*Plenio · Arrange*: chords, what the instrument line plays, energy, key) within the song flow closeness and adds its hints to the writing prompt. The form always stays; below song flow closeness 80 the melody follows the new chords. A plan Plenio cannot use leaves the transcription as it was, and *Song Sheet · Score* says so. The line under the brief says what the chosen mode does; the Song Brief's help page lists the modes.
- **lines under the singing (experimental)** - creative modes only: off (default), the instrument line plays where the voice rests (fills and answers, as in YuE2's own scores); on, it also sounds under the singing - calm, below the voice, never a minor second against it.
- **song flow closeness** (creative modes; *off* ignores it) - 100 exactly the original (nothing is planned, no writer call), 80 chords, key and tempo stay (only sections without an instrument line get one), 50 recognisable (chords recoloured, lines changed; the mode *key change* may lift the key by up to 2; a melody note that clashes with a new chord moves onto it), 20 a free version (new chords and lines, another tempo, the melody written anew over the chords in its old rhythm), 0 only a hint of the original. Default 70.
- **lyrics closeness** (*new lyrics* only) - how close the new lyrics stay to the source's: 0 written without the source's text (default), 1-29 only a hint of it, 30-59 its theme and mood, 60-89 its story in new words, 90-100 its meaning line by line (a singable translation when the language differs). Above 0 the source's lyrics are transcribed for the writer (lyrics ASR), so the source must have clear singing.
- **creativity** - one slider from **0 steady** to **100 experimental**; **50 is the reference**: exactly as before (a workflow saved before the slider existed keeps 50). Away from 40-60 the writer gets rules for the style and the lyrics - plain, regular and typical at the low end; more specific instruments, contrast, fresher images at the high end, and at 81-100 one unexpected but fitting colour. The writer's draft (and a creative mode's plan) gets a lower or higher temperature, and in the templates YuE2's takes are sampled more steadily or more freely (the score comes from the source, so there is no plan to sample). More surprises also bring more misses: try a few seeds. MiniMax Music 3 follows the slider in its writing only.

The brief does not decide which lyrics text is used in the end: that is the job of the **Song Sheet**, where manual text always wins.

Outputs: the brief, a readable summary, *use_source_lyrics* (original lyrics) and *instrumental* - the two switches of the Cover template - and the *song seed* (added to the draft seed: a new value for every version of a series, 0 for one cover). Then every field as its own output - *template, genre, mood, vocals, language, voice, theme, harmony, arrangement* (text), *song flow closeness*, *lyrics closeness*, *creativity* (numbers) and *lines under the singing* (on/off) - with the brief's final values, for example for a tag field of Export Release (which also reads them through its *brief* input as variables). In *new cover every run* a sheet set to *stop for review* stops every new version; the run after *Approve* renders it.

Choose a **voice that fits the melody's range**: YuE2 sings the melody as written. A high (female) melody sung by a "male baritone" became hard to understand in the Phase 4B tests; the Song Sheet warns about it.
