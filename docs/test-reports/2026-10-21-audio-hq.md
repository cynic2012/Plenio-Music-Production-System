## v2.0 - De-Grit and De-Harsh: not shipped

On the nine takes (preset *YuE2 Dense*): -0.3 dB below 350 Hz, -1.4 to -2.7 dB at 350 Hz-1 kHz, -2 to -6 dB above 3.2 kHz; what it removed followed the music (r 0.92-0.98), attacks lost as much as the sustain after them. On the clean synthetic song it took the same off; with known grit the result moved *away* from the truth (SI-SDR 38.5 -> 19.6 dB). Cause: the noise floor was a sliding 18th percentile over 3.5 s, which for held music lies close to the music. A reworked version (floor in the valleys between partials, tonality gate, onset protection) left clean material within 0.3 dB but found almost nothing on the takes (-0.0 to -0.4 dB); even an oracle Wiener mask with the true grit gained only +0.5 dB - noise that follows the music is masked by it. The takes have no stationary floor (the quietest 5 % of frames in the highs: -64 to -95 dBFS). A static EQ with the same average change sounded close to v2 in the A/B material. The owner: do not install it.

## v3.0 - three stages

**FlashSR is two models.** The proposal downloads `YatharthS/FlashSR` (498 624 bytes ONNX, a HierSpeech++ speech upsampler 16 -> 48 kHz; the download was blocked here and not tested). The owner's machine has the other FlashSR (Im & Nam 2025, LAION weights, 3.3 GB, the legacy toolkit's restoration node), which accepts any band-limited input - stage 2 was measured and listened to with that one, run as the legacy node ran it (48 kHz, 5.12 s chunks). Neither its code nor its weights carry a licence (checked on GitHub and Hugging Face, 2026-10-21), so Plenio cannot ship it.

Each stage alone and the chain, median over the nine takes (45 s, the loudest part):

| | stage 1 | stage 2 (FlashSR) | stage 3 | all three |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-3.2 kHz | -0.2 dB | 0.0 dB | -0.9 dB | -0.8 dB |
| 3.2-6 kHz | -0.3 dB | 0.0 dB | -3.1 dB | -3.0 dB |
| 6-10 kHz | -0.4 dB | -1.4 dB | -3.8 dB | -4.9 dB |
| 10-16 kHz | -0.4 dB | -2.6 dB | -4.0 dB | -6.1 dB |
| side/mid above 10.5 kHz | -11.7 dB | +7.9 dB (FlashSR per channel: wider) | +0.2 dB | +6.5 dB |
| attack / sustain | -0.3 / -0.2 dB | -0.8 / -0.6 dB | -3.3 / -3.0 dB | -3.6 / -3.4 dB |
| time per 51 s | 5 s | 54 s | 4 s | 63 s |

- **Stage 1** leaves the tone within half a dB and narrows the side above 6 kHz; its exciter is faint (3rd harmonic -53 dB under a -6 dBFS sine).
- **Stage 2** keeps everything below 6 kHz exactly (-65 dB difference); above the crossover the highs are new (correlation with the original's about 0.3; on the synthetic song as far from the true highs as they are loud).
- **Stage 3** lowers attacks and tails alike, also on the synthetic song without any reverb - a high cut rather than a de-reverb. Its phase lock leaves steady tones untouched (101 dB) and costs about 1 dB of highs in music.
- **The soft clipper** (`tanh` over the whole song when a peak reached 0.999) fired in 6 of 9 takes with stage 1 alone - seven takes peak at 0 dBFS. **The fallback** without the model cut 10-16 kHz by 14.6 dB, silently.

**The owner's verdict** (excerpts of pop, alternative rock and cello & piano, all at -16 LUFS): the original brightest but with artefacts; all three good but rather dull, stage 3 lowering the highs; **stage 1 very good** - artefacts well removed without the highs suffering; **stage 2 also very good**, perhaps even fewer artefacts, the highs hardly suffering; stage 3 dull; a static EQ with the chain's average change worse than stage 1 and slightly washed out. Decision: all three stages in Plenio, 1 and 2 on, 3 bypassed, before the first EQ inside the Master block, the REFINE block outside it removed; presets per stage and, optionally, by genre.

## What ships, and what it measures

Plenio's stages reproduce the proposal's DSP (-140 dB against it on a take) with these changes: no soft clipper, no fallback (HQ 2 needs its model input), numpy instead of torch, and **UniverSR** (MIT code, CC BY 4.0 weights; already in Plenio) as HQ 2's engine at its 16 kHz input condition for the 8.2 kHz crossover. The model's input goes through SciPy's default polyphase filter as in the proposal: with Plenio's steep resampler (flat to 90 % of the new Nyquist frequency) UniverSR generated **no** highs (-107 dB above 8.2 kHz instead of -72 dB).

HQ 2 with UniverSR on the three listening excerpts (standard):

| | pop | alternative rock | cello & piano |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6-10 kHz | -2.2 dB | -1.3 dB | -1.5 dB |
| 10-16 kHz | -4.3 dB | -3.5 dB | -7.5 dB |
| above 16 kHz | +8.2 dB | +0.5 dB | +1.2 dB |
| side/mid above 10.5 kHz, HQ 2 alone | -6.4 dB | -13.0 dB | -3.9 dB |
| side/mid above 10.5 kHz, HQ 1 + HQ 2 | -10.3 dB | -19.1 dB | -7.6 dB |
| time per 51 s | 134 s | 138 s | 136 s |

UniverSR generates both channels alike, so - unlike FlashSR, which made the highs wider - the highs get narrower, and with HQ 1 before it the top octave comes close to mono in the rock take. UniverSR is darker than FlashSR between 10 and 16 kHz and about 2.5x slower. Listening files 7 (HQ 2 with UniverSR) and 8 (HQ 1 + HQ 2, the new template default) went to the owner next to files 1-6. **The owner, 2026-10-22: UniverSR sounds better than FlashSR - no FlashSR any more, switch completely.** HQ 2 is no longer experimental; HQ 3 stays experimental; the profiles *gentle*, *strong* and *wide* are reasoned, not listened to, and say so in their summaries.

## 2026-10-22: the first run in the template, speed, and the release check

**Too little to hear.** The owner's first song through the new template (*1 · YuE2 · Song*, the default
singer-songwriter brief, 144 s) changed audibly very little: *auto* read *singer-songwriter* as *gentle*,
whose HQ 2 started at 12.2 kHz (UniverSR's 24 kHz condition) - the highs above it lost 3.2 dB and nothing
below changed. Fixed: **every profile regenerates from 8.2 kHz** (the setting the owner heard); the profiles
differ only in the share of new highs and the level match; *singer-songwriter* and *acoustic* are no
*gentle* words any more (the default song runs *standard*). HQ 1's *gentle* moved closer to *standard*
(11.5 kHz, 55 %, warmth 0.14).

**Too slow.** HQ 2 took 375.6 s for that song (2.6 s per second of music). Measured on GPU 1, 30 s stereo:

| | time per second of music | highs against the shipped version |
|---|---|---|
| shipped: UniverSR float32, midpoint 4 steps, guidance 1.5, channel by channel | 2.04 s | - |
| both channels in one batch (the same noise) | 2.06 s | identical (-51 dB) |
| bfloat16 autocast | 1.53 s | -25 dB, level +0.3 dB |
| bfloat16, channels-last memory | 1.48 s | -28 dB |
| bfloat16, midpoint 3 steps | 1.12 s | -15 dB, level -1.2 dB |
| bfloat16, midpoint 2 steps | 0.75 s | -6 dB, level -3.5 dB |
| bfloat16, no guidance | 0.93 s | -6 dB, level -5.3 dB |
| bfloat16, Euler 4 steps | 0.75 s | -0.5 dB, level **-19.5 dB** |
| bfloat16, Euler 8 steps | 1.50 s | -3 dB, level -11 dB |
| 10 s chunks instead of 5 s | -7 % | other noise per chunk, same level |

The GPU is saturated by one channel (batching gains nothing), so the model cost stays: 4 midpoint steps
with guidance are 16 network passes per chunk. Shipped: **bfloat16** (within -25 dB of float32) and an
advanced *model steps* field on HQ 2 (default 4; 3 and 2 not listened to). A larger win was outside the
model: HQ 2's 40 ms level envelope (``scipy.ndimage.gaussian_filter1d`` with sigma 1920 samples) took 19.4
of 21 s per 30 s of music - now an FFT convolution with the same numbers (1e-14) in 0.5 s. HQ 2 end to end:
**1.49 s per second of music** (was 2.7); in ComfyUI, the release of the GPU cache moved from every chunk to
the end of the stage. The "6-15 s per song" of a suggestion the owner forwarded is not reachable with this
model on this GPU.

**Release check (1.1.0).** A real run of *1 · YuE2 · Song* on GPU 1 with every real model, queued through
the frontend: 280 s for a 97 s song; HQ 1 and HQ 2 *standard* (auto, singer-songwriter), HQ 2 148 s; EQ,
loudness -14.15 LUFS / -1.2 dBTP; subtitles from Qwen3-ASR with its aligner (13.5 s, 98 % of the lyrics'
words heard); sheet music; no page error. Real-model smokes (HQ, master, cover analysis with Qwen3 and with
Whisper: WER <= 5 % each), 1560 unit/workflow tests, 194 contract/host tests against ComfyUI v0.41.0, 525
frontend tests and the browser check (91 checks, every template, the new HQ chain in the API prompt) pass.
