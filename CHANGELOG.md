## Unreleased

### Added

- **The Song API for other programs: a guide, every field, examples and a reference to browse.** `docs/api/README.md` takes a programmer from the first request to the finished files - quick start in curl, Python and JavaScript, how a song is made (the job's states as a diagram, the stages), every endpoint, the request in detail, following a job, the files, covers, a new take from a release record, every error code with what to do, retries, access from other computers and from web pages, versions and limits. `docs/api/fields.md` lists every field of both templates with its type, default, values and when it applies; `docs/api/examples/` has ready-to-run scripts (Python with the standard library only, Node.js 18+, curl and jq); `docs/api/openapi.json` is the OpenAPI document as a running ComfyUI gives it, browsable on the project's site (`/api/`). The copies are made from the shipped templates by `tools/api_docs.py`, and a test keeps them current.
- **Song API 1.1** (only additions - a program written for 1.0 works unchanged): **a finished song's files to download** - every file of a job's `result.files` has its `url` (`GET /jobs/{id}/files/{name}`, byte ranges work): the audio, the unmastered take, the cover, the sheet music, the subtitles and the release record, only what the record lists; **waiting instead of asking every second** - `GET /jobs/{id}?wait=30` answers as soon as the job's status, place in the queue or stage changes (at most 60 s); **the list filtered** - `GET /jobs?status=queued,running&client=My%20app&template=yue2-song&limit=50`. The OpenAPI document names every call (`operationId`, for client generators), describes every answer (the job, its stage, progress, result and files, the queue, a template's fields) and has examples.
- **A note in every template: which model file goes where.** Like ComfyUI's own templates, each template opens with *Models · what goes where* beside *About this template*: the files it needs and the optional ones, each with its link, size and licence, the folders as a tree under `ComfyUI/models` (with the lyrics ASR Plenio fetches itself), the writer models of your own (GGUF, LM Studio, Ollama …), and for every size of graphics card - below 8 GB to 24 GB and more, and without one - what to choose, with the files for a smaller or a larger card. *0 · System Check* shows the whole set of every template. The notes are made from the model catalogue, so they and the loaders' downloads always agree; the view opens with them in sight.

### Changed

- **The Plenio Player with thousands of songs** (measured with a library of 2958 songs): the library goes compressed to a browser that takes it (9.6 MB -> 1.0 MB) and is answered *unchanged* to a page that has it already (`304`, no body) - a phone in the network loaded all of it at every start; the cards get small covers (a 256-pixel picture: 12 KB instead of a 207 KB cover), made once by Pillow into the player's cache; the page's own scripts go compressed too. Reading the folders again no longer holds up any request: a page, a song's page or the audio that streams get the songs as last read at once, while the folders are read again in the background - before, every request after 15 seconds waited for all records to be read, under one lock, and Live Play asked every two seconds; the folders are walked in one pass that leaves out the folders the library skips (0.34 -> 0.14 s for 44,000 files), the cache of 12 MB is written only when a song came, went or changed, and numbering the titles that repeat no longer takes a time that grows with the square of the library (0.7 s of every reading). While the folders are read the first time, the page hears how far it is.
- The templates' *About* notes name the new *Models* note instead of a short list of their own.

### Fixed

- **The player's page could be shown inside another site's frame**, where a click meant for that site could land on the player's *Remove…*: it now refuses every frame but its own (`X-Frame-Options`, `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors`), and every answer says `nosniff`.
- **A form of another site could make the player read every record again** (*Look for new songs*, `POST /api/rescan`): only the player's own page can now.
- **A request that failed inside the player closed the connection without an answer** - the page saw a network error; it now answers `500` with the reason, and the player's log keeps the details. A request with a wrong `Content-Length` is refused instead of failing.
- **Two requests with one Idempotency-Key at the same time could make two songs** in the Song API; they now make one.
- The Song API's field `source.audio` (a cover's recording) described itself with nothing; it now says how to upload a recording and give its name.

## 1.3.0 - 2026-10-26

**Party songs, a Song API, and videos that change with the song.** The listeners of the Plenio Player make songs of their own: a style, a few choices, *Generate Song* - and when the song is ready it plays in Live Play. Any song of the library is made again from the player - a new take of it, a new song like it or a cover of it -, and whoever opens the player from the network has the whole studio: every setting of the template and of Export Release. Behind it the new Song API on the running ComfyUI makes a song from a template's fields with one HTTP request, for any program, and follows the template when it changes. A video's visuals now follow a plan over the song - one visual, a new one at every section or every few seconds, each chosen in a gallery of moving previews playing to the song's own music - with new looks. And the Song Brief has *sparse vocals*: a song that is mostly instrumental, with a few sung phrases.

- **Party songs**: *Make a song* in the player and *Make your own song* on the guests' phone page - a new song or a cover of an uploaded recording; a vibe, a style template or *Surprise me* fills every field; *Your songs* shows each song's place, a rough time it will be ready and what the studio does now. The fields come from the Song API's description of the template, so a field a later version adds shows by itself; a field shows only while it applies (a voice for sung vocals, the tag fields while tags are written), and one line says how the song will be made. - **The Song API: songs made with one HTTP request** (`/plenio/api/v1` on the running ComfyUI) - for the player and for any other program. `GET /templates/{id}` lists a template's fields - every input of its song groups (*1 · SONG*; the cover's *1 · SOURCE* and *2 · COVER*: the brief's fields, `writer.model`, `finish.hq_mastering`, `finish.cover_art`, `source.audio`, the optional excerpt), read from the template and the running ComfyUI's node definitions every time, so a field a later version adds is in the API at once -, the Export Release's fields and the seeds; `POST /jobs` checks the values against the node definitions (a refusal names the field and why), writes them into the workflow, draws the seeds as the template's *new seed* switch does and queues the run through ComfyUI's own `/prompt`, with the workflow in the record (*Open in ComfyUI* works for it); `GET /jobs/{id}` follows it - queued with its place, running with the stage, succeeded with the record and files, or failed with the node -, `DELETE` cancels; `POST /uploads` takes a cover's recording; an `Idempotency-Key` makes a repeated request one job; `GET /openapi.json` describes it all. The workflow is turned into ComfyUI's API prompt by Plenio itself (subgraphs, promoted widgets, bypassed and muted nodes, DynamicCombo inputs), checked against the prompts ComfyUI's frontend makes of both templates. Review stops are not supported yet; the API is ready for them ([guide](docs/user/concepts/song-api.md), `plenio.core.song_api`, `plenio.core.workflow_prompt`).
- **The Song API makes a song again**: `documents` in a job request go into the Song Sheets as manual, so the run keeps them (a document the template does not keep is refused by name), and `POST /plenio/api/v1/from-record` turns a release record into the request it stands for - the template, the fields, the release fields, the seeds and the documents -, with whether its documents make a new take here and why not, and the values the template no longer takes in `warnings`. Sent without `seeds.take` it makes a new take of the song, without documents and seeds a new song of the same brief ([guide](docs/user/concepts/song-api.md#again-a-new-take-a-new-song-of-the-same-brief)).
- **A video's visuals planned over its time**: one visual all the way, a new one at every section or every few seconds - and for every section or span the visual chosen in a **gallery of little windows, each playing the visual to the music of that part**, or one at random (from the visuals ticked in the visuals' settings, those that suit the song, or every visual; *Shuffle the random ones* draws others; the preview, the strip and the video show the same). The same visual for every chorus and every verse, a change on the beat, other colours at every change; the choices are kept per song.
- **More looks for a video**: the cover behind the visual (blurred, drifting, glowing through its dark), the picture breathing with the beat, how strongly the visuals react, film grain, darker corners, cinema bars, the section's name as it begins, an end card with the cover, the title and who made it, and in from black and out to black.
- **Sparse vocals: a song that is mostly instrumental, with only a few sung phrases.** The Song Brief's *vocals* has a third choice between *sung* and *instrumental*: *sparse vocals* keeps language, voice and theme, and the writer writes only a few short lines that fit the song and carry its melody - a hook phrase, a call, a short chant or wordless sounds (*ooh*, *hey*). It gets a section form of its own: two to four *[Hook]* sections (about one a minute) with one or two lines each among empty *[Instrumental]* sections and a *[Break]* before the last hook - as many sections as a sung song for YuE2 (which plays the empty sections as instrumental parts), as many as an instrumental for MiniMax Music 3 (which ends short maps early), whose caption says that the voice sings only now and then. At most two lines per hook: 6 for 3:00, where a sung YuE2 song has about 22 and a MiniMax one 36. With a score of its own (the DAW template) the score's sections stay and the words go into one to three of them. The draft and the Song Sheet warn when the lyrics have more than twice as many lines, and MiniMax's check for too few lines leaves such a song alone. Templates take `vocals: sparse vocals`; the player's *Vocals* filter has *sparse* (`vocals:sparse`). How the music models plan and sing such lyrics has not been measured yet (`core.lyrics.sparse_form`, `sparse_line_limit`).

### Changed

- **Lyrics fidelity renders a further take only when it is needed** (runs with *high* or *highest* no longer take twice as long for a take that already sings its lyrics). *high* renders the second take only when the first sang less than 96 % of its lyrics' words as written, *highest* the second and the third the same way: a take of 96 % is close enough, and the run goes on with it at once. Before, *high* always rendered the second take and *highest* a third when neither reached 95 % (`core.fidelity.TARGET`).
- **A video fades in from black and out to black** by default (*The look*; switch it off for the old cut).
- **Several values in one filter row of the player**: the chips of one row are alternatives - *jazz* and *pop* keep the songs of either genre, two tempo ranges both tempos -, the rows must all match, and a chip that leaves songs out always does. Tempo, length and creativity, one choice at a time before, take several too; only the Plenio version stays one choice (*from 0.8.0* already holds every newer version). A row counts without its own chips, so its other values say how many songs they add. Two chips of one row, which kept only the songs with both before, now keep the songs with either - saved filters too.

## 1.2.1 - 2026-10-24

**Lyrics sung as written, and heard more exactly.** A new choice in the brief, *lyrics fidelity*, renders a second or a third take and keeps the one that sings most of its lyrics as written. Export hears the lyrics on the take's vocal stem, so the subtitles, the lyrics as sung and the player's following are timed more exactly; lyrics written in Latin letters for a language with a script of its own (Hindi, Korean, Japanese, Russian, Greek) are matched with what was heard; a YuE2 plan that leaves a sung section without notes gets a second plan. In covers the writer model corrects the words the transcription misheard before the render; *Check Sung Words*, which changed a take's words after the export, is retired.

- **Lyrics fidelity**: *standard* (one take), *high - best of 2 takes*, *highest - best of 3 takes* (a third take only when neither sang 95 % of the words): **Best Take** keeps the take that sings most of its lyrics as written. Higher fidelity takes longer.
- **The lyrics heard on the vocal stem**: words timed where nobody sings 2.5 % -> 0.3 %, more words on their sung onset.
- **Fix Misheard Words** in covers: the transcribed original lyrics corrected before the render - only where the writer is sure and the word sounds alike; a cover's lyrics are heard on the recording and its vocal stem.
- **Fixed**: lyrics in Latin letters for another script were heard 0 % (now 92 % in the songs measured); a plan that gave a verse no notes left the verse out.

### Added

- **Fix Misheard Words: a cover's original lyrics corrected before the render** (a word the transcription misheard should become the word most likely sung - only in covers, only in the original lyrics the ASR wrote, never in lyrics the user wrote or a generated song's). A new block in the cover template's *4 · LYRICS*, between *Transcribe Lyrics* and the Song Sheet: the template's writer model reads the draft, its lines numbered and the words the ASR heard with a probability below 0.5 marked, and names the words it is sure were misheard, each with the word most likely sung. Plenio takes a correction only when the writer is sure enough (80 % for a marked word, 90 % for one heard clearly), the words it changes sound like the ones heard (their letters, and their consonants alone, compared: 0.6 or more) and have as many syllables - the melody's notes stay -, and no word then stands twice in a row where the line had none. Song Sheet · Text lists every correction beside the word heard (*Corrected as misheard*); the summary also names those not taken and why. Measured with Gemma 4 E4B on the drafts of 20 songs: it named 190 corrections, 10 held - misheard words (*Zingway* -> *Sing way*, *in'cause* -> *because*, *willing* -> *feeling*); without the sound check it also took rewrites (*it* -> *this*, *not* -> *and*), and Chinese and Japanese characters it swapped at will, so those lyrics are not checked. Only a Cover Brief with *original lyrics* asks the writer - new lyrics, an instrumental and lyrics made manual in the sheet (the sheet then does not even ask for the draft) are never changed, and no model loads for them (`core.heard_check`; Compose Misheard Check, Apply Misheard Check; Transcribe Lyrics' report now lists the heard words of every draft line with their probability).
- **A cover's original lyrics from the recording or its vocal stem** (the vocal stem only where it hears better). *Sung Pitch* hands its vocal stem on, and *Transcribe Lyrics* hears both and keeps the hearing it is more confident of (the mean log probability of its words, the stem only when clearly ahead, `core.asr.STEM_MARGIN`). Measured on 15 songs against their lyrics: the stem alone heard worse in 8 and better in 4 (word error 30.0 % against 28.9 % from the mix - a separation leaves artefacts that get misheard); the more confident hearing was the better one in 13 and gave 27.3 % (the better one every time: 26.7 %). The summary says which was kept.
- **Lyrics fidelity: one take, or the best of two or three** (the written and the sung lyrics as close as possible). A music model does not always sing its lyrics - in 37 sung YuE2 takes the median take sang 89 % of its words as written, one in six less than 75 %, and the same documents sang a verse 8 of 65 words in one take and 64 of 65 in the next. Song Brief and Cover Brief have a new last field, **lyrics fidelity**: *standard* renders one take, as before; *high - best of 2 takes* renders a second take with the next seed, and the new node **Best Take · lyrics fidelity** keeps the one that sings more of the lyrics as written, as the lyrics ASR hears it (scored like the record's *as sung*); *highest - best of 3 takes* adds a third take when neither sang 95 % of the words. The retakes are lazy chains inside *YuE2 Render*, *YuE2 Takes* and *MiniMax Render*: they render only when Best Take asks for them. Higher fidelity makes the generation take longer - up to about twice (high) or three times (highest). Best Take sits in every template's render section (the cover: after Check Vocals), its report goes into the release record; instrumentals always take one take. Stronger text guidance (YuE2's *cfg_scale* 1.2 instead of 1.0) was tried as part of the higher levels and left out: in 12 paired takes of six songs it sang no closer to the lyrics (4 better, 3 worse, 5 alike), while the better of two seeds raised the mean from 87 % to 91 %. A workflow saved before keeps *standard*, and its blocks are still brought up to date (`core.fidelity`, `core.blocks` takes outputs and nodes added after the old ones).

### Changed

- **The lyrics are heard on the take's vocal stem** (the timing of the vocals as exact as possible). Export Release separates the take's vocals (BS-RoFormer, the model of Stems and Sung Pitch, with an eighth of a chunk overlapping: about 30 s for four minutes on a GPU, the stem 23 dB from the full separation's) and the lyrics ASR listens to them for the subtitles, the lyrics as sung and the player's following. Measured on 20 songs against the vocals' own onsets and silence: words timed where nobody sings 2.5 % -> 0.3 % (one song 16 % -> 0.5 %), words within 0.3 s of a sung onset 86.7 % -> 88.8 %, within 0.15 s 70.4 % -> 72.8 %. Timing the mix's words on the stem was tried and dropped (more lines left untimed). *lyrics heard on* (advanced) chooses *vocal stem* (default) or *the song* (quicker); without the separation model, or when it fails, the song is heard and the summary says why. The record says what was heard (`timing[].heard_from`).
- **The lyrics as sung stay as heard**: 1.2.0's *Check Sung Words*, a block after Export Release in the song and cover templates, is gone from the templates - correcting a generated song's sung words against its lyrics changes what the take really sings. Its two nodes stay, deprecated and doing nothing, so a workflow saved with the block still runs; a song it corrected keeps its corrections listed.

### Fixed

- **Lyrics written in Latin letters for a language with a script of its own** (songs whose sung lyrics looked completely different from their lyrics). The Bollywood template's writer writes Hindi as Hinglish (*Dil se dil tak*); the takes sang every line, but Qwen3-ASR hears Hindi in Devanagari, so not one heard word paired with a lyrics word - heard 0 %, the sung lyrics shown in another script, the subtitles without times. Export Release now writes such heard words in Latin letters the way the lyrics spell them (Devanagari, Hangul, Kana, Cyrillic and Greek; Chinese characters need a dictionary and stay) and compares both in a looser spelling (*raasta* and *rasta*, *mein* and *men*); a word that pairs is shown as the lyrics write it (`core.romanize`). In two such songs 92 % of the words were heard instead of none. Songs exported before keep their record until they are exported again.
- **A planned section without notes for its words** (songs that skipped a verse). YuE2's plan sometimes gives a sung section almost no Vocal notes - one verse had a single note for 62 syllables, and the take left the verse out and began with the chorus. In 21 sung YuE2 songs the words of sections with fewer than 0.3 notes per syllable were heard at 34 %, all others at 88-90 %. *Match Song Form* now counts the notes too: such a plan (a section of about two lines or more) asks YuE2 for a second plan, and when that one is no better, the thin section takes the melody of a section of its kind with notes for its words (*sparse*, *fill* in its report; 2-7 % of the sung songs measured had one).
