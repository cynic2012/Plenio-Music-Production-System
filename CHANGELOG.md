## 1.0.0 - 2026-10-16

**Plenio 1.0.** The whole way from an idea to a song people listen to, on your own computer and free: a local language model writes the song (or you compose it yourself), YuE2 or MiniMax Music 3 performs it, Plenio masters it and exports it with its tags, its lyrics as subtitles and its whole project - and now the **Plenio Player** plays it, shows it and shares it. After fourteen published versions in three weeks, the templates, the nodes and the release record have settled: 1.0.0 is the version to build on, and saved workflows and release records keep working in every 1.x version. (0.8.1 was not published; everything it had is in 1.0.0.)

- **The Plenio Player**: your songs in the browser, without ComfyUI - one click from ComfyUI, search, a page per song, the lyrics lit up as they are sung and the sheet music following the music, playlists, Winamp-style visuals.
- **Plenio Drop**: MilkDrop-style visuals of Plenio's own on the graphics card - 16 presets, one made up anew each time, and presets of your own as a short JSON file.
- **Live Play**: a playlist for a party that you change while it plays; the songs you make in ComfyUI join it as soon as they are exported.
- **A song as a video**: an MP4 of the visual with the lyrics (subtitles or karaoke), the notation following the music and a title card - Full HD, 4K, square or upright for Shorts.
- **Share your songs as a player**: one HTML file with the player and the songs as MP3 - anyone double-clicks it and listens, with lyrics, notation and visuals, no install.
- **Lyrics subtitles timed by listening**, tags filled from the brief with variables, seeds with one plain switch, wider briefs and short tooltips beside the node.

## 0.8.0 - 2026-10-15

The song's FLAC carries its whole project: drop it onto ComfyUI - or open it in *Continue a Plenio song* - and you are back in the workflow it was made with. A free cover gets a new melody over the writer's chords: below *song flow closeness* 80 the melody follows the new harmony - from 50 the notes that clash move, below 50 Plenio writes the melody anew in its old rhythm. The writer sees every note of a bar, the harmony guard knows more clashes, and motifs move along the key's scale.

### Added

- **The project inside the FLAC** (GitHub issue #9): Export Release's new **project in FLAC** (on by default) writes the release record - the workflow, the approved Song Sheets, score, lyrics, style, seeds and settings - into the song's FLAC, gzip-compressed in a metadata block of its own that players skip (a FLAC *APPLICATION* block, id `PLNO`, layout `plenio.project/1`): about 25 KB for a typical record of 300 KB (at most 35 KB in 856 songs measured). **File > Continue a Plenio song…** opens a song from its FLAC (*Open a record or FLAC…*), and so does a FLAC dropped onto the empty canvas - the browser reads the record from the file's metadata, nothing is uploaded; a FLAC without a record, one dropped onto a node or with Shift held goes to ComfyUI as before. The `.plenio.json` next to the song stays. A file cannot hold its own hash: the record lists a FLAC that carries it with the size and SHA-256 it has without the record (`"project": "embedded"`), so the copy in the FLAC and the `.plenio.json` are the same record, and two renders with the same audio and tags list the same hash; every FLAC entry also has `audio_md5`, the MD5 of the decoded audio from the file's STREAMINFO. Secrets stay redacted as in the record; turn the option off for files you share without their making-of (ComfyUI's `--disable-metadata` turns it off too).

### Changed

- **A cover's melody follows the new chords** (owner's decision): below *song flow closeness* 80 the melody no longer stays frozen while the writer re-harmonises. From 50 a melody note that clashes with a new chord moves onto it (the nearest chord tone on a strong beat or held for a beat, a step otherwise); below 50 Plenio writes the melody anew over the chords - chord tones on the accents, small steps, phrases that end on a chord tone, near the old line at 49 and freer down to 0 (the arrangement seed picks the phrase shape). A sung melody keeps its rhythm, so its syllables stay on their notes; an instrumental's lead line may also get new rhythms below 45 (from 20 its first bar keeps its rhythm). The lower the closeness, the less the old melody holds the writer's chords back - at 20 they win nearly everywhere. The form always stays; from 80, and in songs, the melody stays as before.
- **The writer sees the whole bar's melody**: the score table of *Compose Arrangement* and the re-ask showed only the notes on beats 1 and 3, while the harmony guard weighs every note of the bar - now every note on a beat or an eighth long is shown, those on a strong beat marked (`E* G A* B`).
- **More clashes recognised**: the harmony guard also rejects a melody note a half step under a chord's major seventh (Bb against Cmaj7), the #4 beside a chord's fifth (F# over C) and, on a strong beat or held, a minor third over a major triad - blues, rock and metal keep their blue third as a short passing note, and over a dominant seventh chord (the blues' I7) also held. The instrument lines follow the same rules.
- **Motifs move along the key's scale**: a *motif* figure moved onto another chord keeps its scale steps (C E G over C becomes A C E over Am - before, it moved by semitones and sang A C# E), takes the chord's own tone where the chord alters the scale (G# over E in A minor), and no motif, line or countermelody note clashes on beats 2 and 4 any more.
- **Rests in the notation**: the score editor and the sheet music draw a rest that runs over the middle of an even bar as two (`c2z6z6z2` instead of a dotted half rest hiding beat 3). The score YuE2 reads keeps its own way of writing rests.
