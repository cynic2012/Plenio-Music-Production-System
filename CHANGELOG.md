## Unreleased

### Added

- **Fix Misheard Words: a cover's original lyrics corrected before the render** (owner's rule 2026-10-24: a word the transcription misheard should become the word most likely sung - only in covers, only in the original lyrics the ASR wrote, never in lyrics the user wrote or a generated song's). A new block in the cover template's *4 · LYRICS*, between *Transcribe Lyrics* and the Song Sheet: the template's writer model reads the draft, its lines numbered and the words the ASR heard with a probability below 0.5 marked, and names the words it is sure were misheard, each with the word most likely sung. Plenio takes a correction only when the writer is sure enough (80 % for a marked word, 90 % for one heard clearly), the words it changes sound like the ones heard (their letters, and their consonants alone, compared: 0.6 or more) and have as many syllables - the melody's notes stay -, and no word then stands twice in a row where the line had none. Song Sheet · Text lists every correction beside the word heard (*Corrected as misheard*); the summary also names those not taken and why. Measured with Gemma 4 E4B on the drafts of 20 of the owner's songs: it named 190 corrections, 10 held - misheard words (*Zingway* -> *Sing way*, *in'cause* -> *because*, *willing* -> *feeling*); without the sound check it also took rewrites (*it* -> *this*, *not* -> *and*), and Chinese and Japanese characters it swapped at will, so those lyrics are not checked. Only a Cover Brief with *original lyrics* asks the writer - new lyrics, an instrumental and lyrics made manual in the sheet (the sheet then does not even ask for the draft) are never changed, and no model loads for them (`core.heard_check`; Compose Misheard Check, Apply Misheard Check; Transcribe Lyrics' report now lists the heard words of every draft line with their probability).
- **A cover's original lyrics from the recording or its vocal stem** (owner 2026-10-24: "the vocal stem for the transcription only where it brings improvements"). *Sung Pitch* hands its vocal stem on, and *Transcribe Lyrics* hears both and keeps the hearing it is more confident of (the mean log probability of its words, the stem only when clearly ahead, `core.asr.STEM_MARGIN`). Measured on 15 songs against their lyrics: the stem alone heard worse in 8 and better in 4 (word error 30.0 % against 28.9 % from the mix - a separation leaves artefacts that get misheard); the more confident hearing was the better one in 13 and gave 27.3 % (the better one every time: 26.7 %). The summary says which was kept.

### Changed

- **The lyrics are heard on the take's vocal stem** (owner 2026-10-24: the timing of the vocals as good as at all possible). Export Release separates the take's vocals (BS-RoFormer, the model of Stems and Sung Pitch, with an eighth of a chunk overlapping: about 30 s for four minutes on a GPU, the stem 23 dB from the full separation's) and the lyrics ASR listens to them for the subtitles, the lyrics as sung and the player's following. Measured on 20 of the owner's songs against the vocals' own onsets and silence: words timed where nobody sings 2.5 % -> 0.3 % (one song 16 % -> 0.5 %), words within 0.3 s of a sung onset 86.7 % -> 88.8 %, within 0.15 s 70.4 % -> 72.8 %. Timing the mix's words on the stem was tried and dropped (more lines left untimed). *lyrics heard on* (advanced) chooses *vocal stem* (default) or *the song* (quicker); without the separation model, or when it fails, the song is heard and the summary says why. The record says what was heard (`timing[].heard_from`).
- **The lyrics as sung stay as heard** (owner's rule 2026-10-24): 1.2.0's *Check Sung Words*, a block after Export Release in the song and cover templates, is gone from the templates - correcting a generated song's sung words against its lyrics changes what the take really sings. Its two nodes stay, deprecated and doing nothing, so a workflow saved with the block still runs; a song it corrected keeps its corrections listed.

### Fixed

- **Lyrics written in Latin letters for a language with a script of its own** (owner's report 2026-10-24: songs whose sung lyrics were "completely different"). The Bollywood template's writer writes Hindi as Hinglish (*Dil se dil tak*); the takes sang every line, but Qwen3-ASR hears Hindi in Devanagari, so not one heard word paired with a lyrics word - heard 0 %, the sung lyrics shown in another script, the subtitles without times. Export Release now writes such heard words in Latin letters the way the lyrics spell them (Devanagari, Hangul, Kana, Cyrillic and Greek; Chinese characters need a dictionary and stay) and compares both in a looser spelling (*raasta* and *rasta*, *mein* and *men*); a word that pairs is shown as the lyrics write it (`core.romanize`). In the owner's two such songs 92 % of the words were heard instead of none. Songs exported before keep their record until they are exported again.
- **A planned section without notes for its words** (same report). YuE2's plan sometimes gives a sung section almost no Vocal notes - one verse had a single note for 62 syllables, and the take left the verse out and began with the chorus. In the owner's sung songs of 2026-10-24 the words of sections with fewer than 0.3 notes per syllable were heard at 34 %, all others at 88-90 %. *Match Song Form* now counts the notes too: such a plan (a section of about two lines or more) asks YuE2 for a second plan, and when that one is no better, the thin section takes the melody of a section of its kind with notes for its words (*sparse*, *fill* in its report; 2-7 % of the owner's sung songs had one).

### Added

- **The lyrics as written and as sung** (owner's request 2026-10-23: the lyrics that were wanted and the ones the take really sings, both to follow and both documented). A music model does not always sing its lyrics - it repeats a line, makes one up, leaves one out. Export Release already listens to every take; it now also keeps **what the take sings**, as heard: laid out like the lyrics wherever the take sings them (their lines, section tags and spelling where the same word was heard), the words they do not have in lines of their own (`core.subtitles.time_sung`). It goes into `<name> (sung).srt` and the release record (`timing[].sung`: the text, the timed lines, the share of the lyrics' words heard, the words heard that they do not have, the lines not heard, per section), and the summary says per take how far it follows its lyrics and names a section not sung as written. The **Plenio Player** follows either: *As written* or *As sung* on the song's *Lyrics* tab, in 🎤 in Now playing and in *Settings > Lyrics* (kept for every song; a song exported before shows its written lyrics), and *Save as a video* has the same choice. The notation always follows the written lyrics' syllables.
- **The lyrics a song shows: as sung where its take departs far from them** (owner's request 2026-10-23). By default (*Auto*) a song whose take sings less than 75 % of its lyrics' words as written, or more than a quarter of words of its own, shows its lyrics **as sung**, the others **as written**. Switch a song either way like master and original - the **Lyrics · as written / as sung** chip in the player bar (key **W**), the song's *Lyrics* tab, 🎤 in Now playing - and the choice is kept for that song; *Settings > Lyrics* sets the rule (*Auto*, *As written*, *As sung*). *Save as a video* starts with what the song shows.
- **Check Sung Words: misheard words corrected** (owner's request 2026-10-23). A new block after Export Release in the song and cover templates: the writer model reads the lyrics and every line the take sings, as heard, with the words the lyrics do not have there marked, and names those it is sure were misheard, each with the word most likely sung. Plenio takes a correction only when it replaces a whole marked bracket (one word, or several heard in a row - *a detour* heard as *ready to*), the writer is at least 80 % sure, the words have as many syllables as what was heard and no word stands twice in a row where the line had none; ad-libs and words the music model chose stay. On a song with two planted mishearings Gemma 4 12B and Qwen3.5 9B both found them (*rowed* -> *road*, *ready to* -> *A detour*); Qwen3.5 9B also changed a sung *I* into *If* - how good it is depends on the writer model, and the record keeps what was heard. The corrections go into the release record's sung lines and text - each kept with the word heard (`timing[].sung.corrected`) - and into `(sung).srt`; the player lists them on the song's page. Nothing runs (no writer model loads) for a song without sung lyrics or without such words, and a record is checked once (`core.sung_check`; Compose Sung Check, Apply Sung Check; a lock keeps it from crossing the sheet music PDF's update of the same record).
- **A login for the Plenio Player** (owner's request 2026-10-23: the same likes, playlists and plays on every device). The player opens with *Who is listening?*: a **user** (picture, then password) keeps their own playlists, favourites, plays, tags and settings on the player's computer - the same on every device that opens the player, also by the network link; the **admin** logs in only on the player's own computer, has the player's state as it was and sets up the users in *Settings > Accounts* (name, password, picture, new password, rename, remove); a **guest** keeps everything in that browser, as before. A device stays logged in and a guest's choice is remembered, so the page asks once; the name and picture in the sidebar log out. Passwords are kept only as PBKDF2-SHA256 hashes (`users.json` beside the state, each user's things in `users/<id>/`), sessions as hashes of their random tokens (an HttpOnly cookie); eight wrong passwords from one device in ten minutes make it wait (`plenio.player.accounts`).
- **Play the MP3** (owner's request 2026-10-23): *Settings > Sound* - a song exported as FLAC (or WAV) and MP3 plays its MP3, about a sixth of the data over the network.
- **HQ Mastering & Cover Art** (owner's request 2026-10-23): the switch at the top of every template is now two cards with an icon - **HQ Mastering** and **Cover Art** -, wide enough for what they say; a click switches one, in the graph and in App mode. The Cover Art card bypasses the template's three cover nodes (no image model loads) and shows a cover bypassed by hand too.

### Fixed

- **A click on the Plenio Player button waits only for its own player**: the launcher could wait for a player it had started for another state folder, or one quit a moment before; it now waits only for one of the same state and ports, and starts its own when the one it waited for has stopped.
- **The Plenio Player starts every time** (owner's report 2026-10-23: with well over a thousand songs it sometimes said it could not start, and a second or third click brought it up). The player read all its records before it took its port, so ComfyUI's button - waiting 25 seconds - did not find it, said so, and a further click started a second player that found the port taken (seen in the owner's player.log). ComfyUI starts one player at a time - a click while one starts waits for it -, a port a busy player holds no longer counts as free, and the button waits up to a minute. - **The notation cursor follows the music, through the instrumental parts too** (owner's report 2026-10-23: the cursor raced through an instrumental part, then waited at the lyrics). The cursor ran straight from one measured syllable to the next; between syllables more than 4 s apart it ran more than 30 % off the score's tempo in 85 % of the time (on the owner's newest covers) - and a word heard in the intro, or a line the take did not sing, pulled it far ahead. The player now finds the score's path through the recording frame by frame (dynamic time warping over the pitch classes of the score's notes and of the music, near the measured warp; `plenio.player.align.follow`, kept in the player's cache): where the take holds, where it leaves part of its plan out. Measured on 20 of the owner's songs against their vocal stems: the score's vocal notes within 0.15 s of a sung onset 75 % (the syllables: 69 %), their time on the voice 99 % (94 %); a 5 s cut out of an instrumental is found. Songs exported before 1.0.0 follow it too.
- **A style draft no longer stops a run that goes through** (owner's report 2026-10-23: about half of a cover series failed with *The style contains timing or duration*). The check took *warm, driving second half* - a writer's description of the song's build - for a length; a unit counts as a length only after a number now (*3 minutes*, *a 30-second intro*, *2:45*). And a **drafted** style with an error its engine fixes by itself goes on corrected, with a warning that names what was removed (timing, ABC lines, bracketed tags, descriptors past 120 words; for instrumentals vocals and languages) - the user's own style is never changed. Over the 1 081 styles in the owner's writer cache, one error remains (193 words), and it is cut.
- **A writer's answer without headings and without lyrics is read** (a cover with its original lyrics: Qwen3.8 27B answered with the title, the style, *none* and the artwork, and the run stopped with *no style block*).
- **The lyrics follow the song again** (owner's report 2026-10-23: the following of the lyrics, in the sheet and in the lyrics, had got worse - often far from what was sung). Since 1.1.0 the lyrics ASR heard a song in one piece of up to four minutes, and over a song with its music Qwen3-ForcedAligner loses its place: a word stretched over the first 24 seconds, a third of the words of zero length, the rest crowded together - on the owner's songs of 1.1.0 four in ten syllables lay more than 2 s from their place in the score (1.0: 2 %), lines up to a minute early. Qwen3 now hears a song in pieces of at most 20 s, cut at quiet moments and each heard with 1.5 s of its neighbours; where two pieces heard the same words at a cut, they are paired by their text and kept once (`workers.asr.owned_words`). Measured on 15 of the owner's YuE2 songs against faster-whisper, an independent timing: 6 % of the words more than 3 s from Whisper's (one piece: 29 %), 72 % of the word starts within 0.15 s of an onset of the separated vocals (59 %). Results heard in one piece are not used again (ASR cache version 2): export a song again to measure it anew.
- **Chinese and Japanese lyrics are matched to what was heard** (the owner's songs in Chinese and Japanese: almost none of their words had times). Their lines have no spaces, so a whole phrase was one word; every Kana and Han character is now a token - in the lyrics and in what was heard.
- **A YuE2 plan with a line of other measures no longer stops the run** (the owner's batch: one song of 44 ended in Score Tools with *group 4, Ins: expected 1–4 measures after expanding Z rests* - YuE2 had planned `Z3` for `Z2`, five measures against four): the plan is repaired before it is prepared - surplus whole-bar rests of the longer voice go, the shorter voice is filled with rests where notes stand over, a line of more than four measures becomes several (`native.repair_groups`); no note is lost, the changes are listed in Score Tools' summary.

## 1.1.0 - 2026-10-23

**Audio HQ, a new ear for the lyrics, and the score in and out.** Every take now goes through **Audio HQ** before the EQ: *Stereo Cleanup* calms the unsteady stereo shimmer of AI renders and *Regenerate Highs* lets UniverSR generate fresh highs above 8.2 kHz - presets that follow the song's genre, in every template's Master block and in Enhance & Master. UniverSR about doubles a song's time, so it runs only with the **HQ Mastering** switch at the top of every template on (off by default). The lyrics are heard by **Qwen3-ASR** and timed word by word by **Qwen3-ForcedAligner**, for covers and for the lyrics subtitles; faster-whisper stays as the fallback for other languages. The System Check says where every model file and asset goes.

- **Audio HQ**: HQ 1 · Stereo Cleanup on, HQ 2 · Regenerate Highs with **HQ mastering** on (off by default), HQ 3 · De-Reverb bypassed; presets *auto* (from the brief's genre), *standard*, *gentle*, *strong*, *wide*, *custom*; about 1.5 s per second of music for HQ 2 on an RTX 5060 Ti.
- **Qwen3-ASR** with its forced aligner is the lyrics ASR (Transcribe Lyrics, subtitles); Plenio carries its inference code and installs nothing.
- **All audio work after the render lives in the Master block**; the REFINE block left the templates; the UniverSR file (229 MB) is needed only for HQ mastering.
- **System Check**: folders, download links and the Plenio assets with their folders.
- **MusicXML round trip**: *Import MusicXML…* reads back what *Export MusicXML* wrote - with the lyrics on their notes, also after a script changed them; other MusicXML is refused.
- **A whole track an octave down or up**; **the bass and the vocals of saved stems as MIDI**; **covers of one song get titles of their own**; **the Plenio Player follows the score's times** where a song has no measured lyrics times, and says why.


### Added

- **Audio HQ: three refinement stages before the EQ, in every template** (owner's request, after a listening test of the *Plenio-AudioHQ v3.0* proposal on YuE2 takes). Inside the **Plenio · Master** block the take now runs through **HQ 1 · Stereo Cleanup** (a high-cut and a spectral cleanup of the *side* signal above 2 kHz - the unsteady stereo shimmer of AI renders - and a little 2x oversampled harmonic warmth; the middle is not filtered), **HQ 2 · Regenerate Highs** (*erase and regenerate*: the UniverSR model generates new highs from the song without its top octaves, and above 8.2 kHz they replace most of the original's at the original's level; below the crossover nothing changes - with the **HQ Mastering** switch on, see below) and **HQ 3 · De-Reverb** (the short metallic room and a phase lock - bypassed: it also lowers the highs by 3-4 dB), then the EQ and Loudness & Dynamics as before. **4 · Enhance & Master** has the three stages as plain nodes, with their presets in App mode. Every stage has the same presets - **auto** (the default: the brief's genre picks the profile), *standard - pop, hip-hop, soul, world*, *gentle - classical, jazz, folk*, *strong - rock, metal, punk*, *wide - electronic, dance, ambient* - and *custom* for its fields; the summary says which profile ran and why (`strong (auto: genre 'thrash metal' (metal))`). **HQ 2 regenerates from 8.2 kHz in every preset** - the setting the owner listened to (a first build let *gentle* start at 12.2 kHz, and the templates' default singer-songwriter song changed too little to hear). *standard* is the proposal the owner listened to - HQ 2 with UniverSR, which sounded better than the proposal's FlashSR (Plenio offers no FlashSR); the other profiles are marked provisional. The code follows the proposal's DSP (checked to -140 dB) without its whole-song soft clipper and without its silent fallback for a missing model (it cut the highs by 15 dB). **Time:** HQ 2 takes about 1.5 s per second of music on an RTX 5060 Ti - UniverSR in bfloat16, and the 40 ms level envelope as an FFT convolution (it took 19 of 21 seconds per 30 s of music); its advanced *model steps* trade highs for time (3: about 1.1 s, 2: about 0.75 s). Guide: [Audio HQ](docs/user/concepts/audio-hq.md); measurements: [test report](docs/test-reports/2026-10-21-audio-hq.md).
- **Qwen3-ASR is the lyrics ASR** (owner's request): **Qwen3-ASR 1.7B** hears the sung words and **Qwen3-ForcedAligner 0.6B** gives every word its time - in *Transcribe Lyrics* (covers with the original lyrics, *Check sung lyrics*) and in Export Release's lyrics subtitles, so the subtitles take the aligner's exact times instead of Whisper's corrected ones. The two models run one after the other in Plenio's ASR worker (about 4 GB of VRAM at a time; a four-minute song in about half a minute on an RTX 5060 Ti) and come as Plenio assets (Apache-2.0, 4.1 + 1.8 GB, fetched on first use). transformers supports the model only from 5.13 on and ComfyUI ships an older 5.x, so Plenio carries its inference code (`plenio/third_party/qwen3_asr`, vendored from transformers 5.17 by `tools/vendor_qwen3_asr.py`; the same words and times as transformers 5.17 on a test song) and installs nothing. **faster-whisper large-v3 stays as the fallback**: for a language the aligner does not know (it times English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Chinese, Cantonese, Japanese and Korean) Whisper takes over by itself and the summary says so; *Transcribe Lyrics* still offers it, so workflows saved with it keep running. Word confidence (the editor's unsure words) comes from the transcription's token probabilities.
- **Import MusicXML…** in the score editor (a user's request: export, let a script put the lyrics note by note, import again). It reads **only Plenio's own MusicXML** - the file *Export MusicXML* wrote, changed or not - and refuses any other (a notation program's, a third part, chords inside a part, a second voice, grace notes, tuplets, `<backup>`, a bar that does not hold its meter, entity declarations ...) with the bar and the reason. The score comes back exactly (also its line groups and section names); when the file's words are the sheet's, the lyrics stay as they are and every line is sung on the notes the file gives it, else the lyrics are written from the file's lines and blocks. The export now marks the lyrics' line and block ends and block tags (`<end-line/>`, `<end-paragraph/>`, `<lyric name>`) and keeps the line groups and section names. A lyrics line placed by hand can carry the onset of every syllable (or word) after its span - the lyrics lane keeps them while the line stays where it is. Measured on the owner's 2230 scores: every score, its ABC text and every lyrics line's placement came back exactly.
- **A whole Vocal or Instrument track an octave down or up** (a user's request; until now only the selected notes): *Whole score* in the palette and **−8va / +8va** in the DAW layout's track headers - one undo step, the chord symbols and keys stay.
- **The bass and the vocals as MIDI** (a user's request: "more MIDI tracks - bass or other lead instruments"): a saved **bass** or **vocals** stem is also written as `bass.mid` / `vocals.mid` next to its FLAC - the notes where the stem plays them (pitch by YIN in the instrument's range, a new note at every attack), at the song's tempo. Measured on five renders: 93-100 % of the bass notes' time on a tone of the score's chord. Experimental; the drums and the *other* stem (several notes at once) get none.
- **Covers of one song get titles of their own** (a user's report: in the player every cover was titled the same): with *collision: number* Export Release also numbers a title another song of the folder has - *Yesterday (2)* in the tags, the record and the file name. The Plenio Player shows songs exported earlier with one title as *(2)*, *(3)* … in the order they were made; they stay takes of one song.
- **The Plenio Player follows the score where no lyrics times were measured** (a user's report: "This song was exported without lyrics times"): a song with a score follows the score's times where the player finds the score in the recording (half the lines within 0.15 s, 83 % within a second of the measured times), and the page says why the song has no times of its own - the release record now says whether subtitles were asked for and why they were not made.
- **The HQ Mastering switch** (owner's request: UniverSR about doubles a song's time - measured on *1 · YuE2 · Song*: 97 s of music in 280 s with it, about 130 s without; 103 s of music in 260 s, 157 s of them UniverSR): a node at the top of every template, **off by default**, its labels say what it does (*on: less AI shimmer, 2× time*). On, HQ 2 · Regenerate Highs runs in the Master block; off, HQ 2 passes the song unchanged and its model is **not even loaded** - HQ 2 asks for it only when on, and Load Audio Model accepts a file that is not installed until it is needed. The switch is wired into the Master block alone, so switching it runs only the mastering again. The Master block has an *HQ mastering* input, HQ 2 an *HQ mastering* switch (on for a node you place yourself).
