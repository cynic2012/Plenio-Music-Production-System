## Unreleased

### Added

- **Party songs: guests and users make songs from the Plenio Player.** *Make a song* in the sidebar (and *Make your own song* on the party guests' phone page): a new song or a cover of an uploaded recording; a style - a vibe (*Pop*, *Rock*, *Latin* … - each click another template of it), any style template, or *Surprise me* - that fills every field and the description; genre, mood, tempo, length, vocals, language, voice, theme, key and meter as lists of every value the templates have, each with a value of one's own; **Generate Song**. *Your songs* shows each song in its order - its title once made, before its style, length and voice -, in line with its place and a rough time it will be ready, being made with how long and what the studio does now (*writing the lyrics*, *composing*, *singing and playing* …), or ready with ▶; a waiting song moves past one's next song, is changed or taken out, one being made is stopped. The admin decides in *Settings > Party songs* whether logged-in users and guests may (off by default), how many songs one person may have in the making, the longest length and whether covers may be made. Every party song is made by YuE2 with HQ mastering off and cover art on, in `plenio/Party-Songs-<date>`, album *Party-Song <date>*, artist the user's name or *Guest*; when ready it joins Live Play as every new song does (the player reads its folder also when it is not one of its folders). The songs wait in the player's own line and go to ComfyUI one at a time; the estimates follow how long the songs before took on this computer (measured on an RTX 5060 Ti: a song of about 1:00 in 5-6 minutes). Nothing of it in a shared player (`plenio.player.maker`; the guests' page is a small page of its own, `plenio/player/guest_static`).
- **The Song API: songs made with one HTTP request** (`/plenio/api/v1` on the running ComfyUI) - for the player and for any other program. `GET /templates/{id}` lists a template's fields - every input of its song groups (*1 · SONG*; the cover's *1 · SOURCE* and *2 · COVER*: the brief's fields, `writer.model`, `finish.hq_mastering`, `finish.cover_art`, `source.audio`, the optional excerpt), read from the template and the running ComfyUI's node definitions every time, so a field a later version adds is in the API at once -, the Export Release's fields and the seeds; `POST /jobs` checks the values against the node definitions (a refusal names the field and why), writes them into the workflow, draws the seeds as the template's *new seed* switch does and queues the run through ComfyUI's own `/prompt`, with the workflow in the record (*Open in ComfyUI* works for it); `GET /jobs/{id}` follows it - queued with its place, running with the stage, succeeded with the record and files, or failed with the node -, `DELETE` cancels; `POST /uploads` takes a cover's recording; an `Idempotency-Key` makes a repeated request one job; `GET /openapi.json` describes it all. The workflow is turned into ComfyUI's API prompt by Plenio itself (subgraphs, promoted widgets, bypassed and muted nodes, DynamicCombo inputs), checked against the prompts ComfyUI's frontend makes of both templates. Review stops are not supported yet; the API is ready for them ([guide](docs/user/concepts/song-api.md), `plenio.core.song_api`, `plenio.core.workflow_prompt`).
- **A video's visuals planned over its time**: one visual all the way, a new one at every section or every few seconds - and for every section or span the visual chosen in a **gallery of little windows, each playing the visual to the music of that part**, or one at random (from the visuals ticked in the visuals' settings, those that suit the song, or every visual; *Shuffle the random ones* draws others; the preview, the strip and the video show the same). The same visual for every chorus and every verse, a change on the beat, other colours at every change; the choices are kept per song.
- **More looks for a video**: the cover behind the visual (blurred, drifting, glowing through its dark), the picture breathing with the beat, how strongly the visuals react, film grain, darker corners, cinema bars, the section's name as it begins, an end card with the cover, the title and who made it, and in from black and out to black.

- **Sparse vocals: a song that is mostly instrumental, with only a few sung phrases.** The Song Brief's *vocals* has a third choice between *sung* and *instrumental*: *sparse vocals* keeps language, voice and theme, and the writer writes only a few short lines that fit the song and carry its melody - a hook phrase, a call, a short chant or wordless sounds (*ooh*, *hey*). It gets a section form of its own: two to four *[Hook]* sections (about one a minute) with one or two lines each among empty *[Instrumental]* sections and a *[Break]* before the last hook - as many sections as a sung song for YuE2 (which plays the empty sections as instrumental parts), as many as an instrumental for MiniMax Music 3 (which ends short maps early), whose caption says that the voice sings only now and then. At most two lines per hook: 6 for 3:00, where a sung YuE2 song has about 22 and a MiniMax one 36. With a score of its own (the DAW template) the score's sections stay and the words go into one to three of them. The draft and the Song Sheet warn when the lyrics have more than twice as many lines, and MiniMax's check for too few lines leaves such a song alone. Templates take `vocals: sparse vocals`; the player's *Vocals* filter has *sparse* (`vocals:sparse`). How the music models plan and sing such lyrics has not been measured yet (`core.lyrics.sparse_form`, `sparse_line_limit`).

## 1.2.1 - 2026-10-24

**Lyrics sung as written, and heard more exactly.** A new choice in the brief, *lyrics fidelity*, renders a second or a third take and keeps the one that sings most of its lyrics as written. Export hears the lyrics on the take's vocal stem, so the subtitles, the lyrics as sung and the player's following are timed more exactly; lyrics written in Latin letters for a language with a script of its own (Hindi, Korean, Japanese, Russian, Greek) are matched with what was heard; a YuE2 plan that leaves a sung section without notes gets a second plan. In covers the writer model corrects the words the transcription misheard before the render; *Check Sung Words*, which changed a take's words after the export, is retired.

- **Lyrics fidelity**: *standard* (one take), *high - best of 2 takes*, *highest - best of 3 takes* (a third take only when neither sang 95 % of the words): **Best Take** keeps the take that sings most of its lyrics as written. Higher fidelity takes longer.
- **The lyrics heard on the vocal stem**: words timed where nobody sings 2.5 % -> 0.3 %, more words on their sung onset.
- **Fix Misheard Words** in covers: the transcribed original lyrics corrected before the render - only where the writer is sure and the word sounds alike; a cover's lyrics are heard on the recording and its vocal stem.
- **Fixed**: lyrics in Latin letters for another script were heard 0 % (now 92 % in the songs measured); a plan that gave a verse no notes left the verse out.

### Added

- **Fix Misheard Words: a cover's original lyrics corrected before the render** (a word the transcription misheard should become the word most likely sung - only in covers, only in the original lyrics the ASR wrote, never in lyrics the user wrote or a generated song's). A new block in the cover template's *4 · LYRICS*, between *Transcribe Lyrics* and the Song Sheet: the template's writer model reads the draft, its lines numbered and the words the ASR heard with a probability below 0.5 marked, and names the words it is sure were misheard, each with the word most likely sung. Plenio takes a correction only when the writer is sure enough (80 % for a marked word, 90 % for one heard clearly), the words it changes sound like the ones heard (their letters, and their consonants alone, compared: 0.6 or more) and have as many syllables - the melody's notes stay -, and no word then stands twice in a row where the line had none. Song Sheet · Text lists every correction beside the word heard (*Corrected as misheard*); the summary also names those not taken and why. Measured with Gemma 4 E4B on the drafts of 20 songs: it named 190 corrections, 10 held - misheard words (*Zingway* -> *Sing way*, *in'cause* -> *because*, *willing* -> *feeling*); without the sound check it also took rewrites (*it* -> *this*, *not* -> *and*), and Chinese and Japanese characters it swapped at will, so those lyrics are not checked. Only a Cover Brief with *original lyrics* asks the writer - new lyrics, an instrumental and lyrics made manual in the sheet (the sheet then does not even ask for the draft) are never changed, and no model loads for them (`core.heard_check`; Compose Misheard Check, Apply Misheard Check; Transcribe Lyrics' report now lists the heard words of every draft line with their probability).
- **A cover's original lyrics from the recording or its vocal stem** (the vocal stem only where it hears better). *Sung Pitch* hands its vocal stem on, and *Transcribe Lyrics* hears both and keeps the hearing it is more confident of (the mean log probability of its words, the stem only when clearly ahead, `core.asr.STEM_MARGIN`). Measured on 15 songs against their lyrics: the stem alone heard worse in 8 and better in 4 (word error 30.0 % against 28.9 % from the mix - a separation leaves artefacts that get misheard); the more confident hearing was the better one in 13 and gave 27.3 % (the better one every time: 26.7 %). The summary says which was kept.
- **Lyrics fidelity: one take, or the best of two or three** (the written and the sung lyrics as close as possible). A music model does not always sing its lyrics - in 37 sung YuE2 takes the median take sang 89 % of its words as written, one in six less than 75 %, and the same documents sang a verse 8 of 65 words in one take and 64 of 65 in the next. Song Brief and Cover Brief have a new last field, **lyrics fidelity**: *standard* renders one take, as before; *high - best of 2 takes* renders a second take with the next seed, and the new node **Best Take · lyrics fidelity** keeps the one that sings more of the lyrics as written, as the lyrics ASR hears it (scored like the record's *as sung*); *highest - best of 3 takes* adds a third take when neither sang 95 % of the words. The retakes are lazy chains inside *YuE2 Render*, *YuE2 Takes* and *MiniMax Render*: they render only when Best Take asks for them. Higher fidelity makes the generation take longer - up to about twice (high) or three times (highest). Best Take sits in every template's render section (the cover: after Check Vocals), its report goes into the release record; instrumentals always take one take. Stronger text guidance (YuE2's *cfg_scale* 1.2 instead of 1.0) was tried as part of the higher levels and left out: in 12 paired takes of six songs it sang no closer to the lyrics (4 better, 3 worse, 5 alike), while the better of two seeds raised the mean from 87 % to 91 %. A workflow saved before keeps *standard*, and its blocks are still brought up to date (`core.fidelity`, `core.blocks` takes outputs and nodes added after the old ones).

### Changed

- **The lyrics are heard on the take's vocal stem** (the timing of the vocals as exact as possible). Export Release separates the take's vocals (BS-RoFormer, the model of Stems and Sung Pitch, with an eighth of a chunk overlapping: about 30 s for four minutes on a GPU, the stem 23 dB from the full separation's) and the lyrics ASR listens to them for the subtitles, the lyrics as sung and the player's following. Measured on 20 songs against the vocals' own onsets and silence: words timed where nobody sings 2.5 % -> 0.3 % (one song 16 % -> 0.5 %), words within 0.3 s of a sung onset 86.7 % -> 88.8 %, within 0.15 s 70.4 % -> 72.8 %. Timing the mix's words on the stem was tried and dropped (more lines left untimed). *lyrics heard on* (advanced) chooses *vocal stem* (default) or *the song* (quicker); without the separation model, or when it fails, the song is heard and the summary says why. The record says what was heard (`timing[].heard_from`).
- **The lyrics as sung stay as heard**: 1.2.0's *Check Sung Words*, a block after Export Release in the song and cover templates, is gone from the templates - correcting a generated song's sung words against its lyrics changes what the take really sings. Its two nodes stay, deprecated and doing nothing, so a workflow saved with the block still runs; a song it corrected keeps its corrections listed.

### Fixed

- **Lyrics written in Latin letters for a language with a script of its own** (songs whose sung lyrics looked completely different from their lyrics). The Bollywood template's writer writes Hindi as Hinglish (*Dil se dil tak*); the takes sang every line, but Qwen3-ASR hears Hindi in Devanagari, so not one heard word paired with a lyrics word - heard 0 %, the sung lyrics shown in another script, the subtitles without times. Export Release now writes such heard words in Latin letters the way the lyrics spell them (Devanagari, Hangul, Kana, Cyrillic and Greek; Chinese characters need a dictionary and stay) and compares both in a looser spelling (*raasta* and *rasta*, *mein* and *men*); a word that pairs is shown as the lyrics write it (`core.romanize`). In two such songs 92 % of the words were heard instead of none. Songs exported before keep their record until they are exported again.
- **A planned section without notes for its words** (songs that skipped a verse). YuE2's plan sometimes gives a sung section almost no Vocal notes - one verse had a single note for 62 syllables, and the take left the verse out and began with the chorus. In 21 sung YuE2 songs the words of sections with fewer than 0.3 notes per syllable were heard at 34 %, all others at 88-90 %. *Match Song Form* now counts the notes too: such a plan (a section of about two lines or more) asks YuE2 for a second plan, and when that one is no better, the thin section takes the melody of a section of its kind with notes for its words (*sparse*, *fill* in its report; 2-7 % of the sung songs measured had one).

## 1.2.0 - 2026-10-24

The player has a login, so every listener keeps their own playlists, likes and plays on every device; it can play the MP3, and it has 42 new visuals - twelve classics and thirty after MilkDrop's best. Titles and cover prompts are drawn from the lyrics, ten titles to pick from. Covers and series run through without stopping, and the lyrics and the notation cursor follow the song again. The templates stand in one row: the HQ Mastering and Cover Art cards and the writer model open them (HQ mastering on by default in Enhance & Master), the Song Sheets show what you edit there, the stems sit inside the Master block, and Cover Art above the render.

- **The lyrics as written and as sung**: `(sung).srt` and the record keep what the take sings; the player shows the lyrics as sung where a take departs far from them (switch it with **W**). **Check Sung Words** lets the writer model correct misheard words, only those it is sure of.
- **A login for the Plenio Player**: users with a password (set up by the admin on the player's computer), the admin, or a guest; passwords and sessions kept only as hashes. And *Settings > Sound* plays the MP3.
- **42 new visuals**: Digital Rain, VU Meters, Vinyl, Harmonograph, Spiral Galaxy … and thirty Plenio Drop presets after the MilkDrop classics, with six new warp patterns.

- **Runs that go through**: covers start with *new cover every run*, every seed follows the brief's mode, a drafted style with a fixable error goes on corrected, and a YuE2 plan with a line of other measures is repaired.
- **The templates**: one row of sections of one height; Cover Art on by default, HQ mastering on by default in Enhance & Master; the writer model at the start; the Song Sheets coloured with an edit button that says what you edit; the stems in the Master block behind its *stems* switch.
- **Fixed**: the lyrics follow the song again (Qwen3 hears it in pieces of 20 s), the notation cursor follows the music, Chinese and Japanese lyrics get their times, and the Plenio Player starts every time.


### Added

- **The lyrics as written and as sung** (the lyrics that were wanted and the ones the take really sings, both to follow and both documented). A music model does not always sing its lyrics - it repeats a line, makes one up, leaves one out. Export Release already listens to every take; it now also keeps **what the take sings**, as heard: laid out like the lyrics wherever the take sings them (their lines, section tags and spelling where the same word was heard), the words they do not have in lines of their own (`core.subtitles.time_sung`). It goes into `<name> (sung).srt` and the release record (`timing[].sung`: the text, the timed lines, the share of the lyrics' words heard, the words heard that they do not have, the lines not heard, per section), and the summary says per take how far it follows its lyrics and names a section not sung as written. The **Plenio Player** follows either: *As written* or *As sung* on the song's *Lyrics* tab, in 🎤 in Now playing and in *Settings > Lyrics* (kept for every song; a song exported before shows its written lyrics), and *Save as a video* has the same choice. The notation always follows the written lyrics' syllables.
- **The lyrics a song shows: as sung where its take departs far from them**. By default (*Auto*) a song whose take sings less than 75 % of its lyrics' words as written, or more than a quarter of words of its own, shows its lyrics **as sung**, the others **as written**. Switch a song either way like master and original - the **Lyrics · as written / as sung** chip in the player bar (key **W**), the song's *Lyrics* tab, 🎤 in Now playing - and the choice is kept for that song; *Settings > Lyrics* sets the rule (*Auto*, *As written*, *As sung*). *Save as a video* starts with what the song shows.
- **Check Sung Words: misheard words corrected**. A new block after Export Release in the song and cover templates: the writer model reads the lyrics and every line the take sings, as heard, with the words the lyrics do not have there marked, and names those it is sure were misheard, each with the word most likely sung. Plenio takes a correction only when it replaces a whole marked bracket (one word, or several heard in a row - *a detour* heard as *ready to*), the writer is at least 80 % sure, the words have as many syllables as what was heard and no word stands twice in a row where the line had none; ad-libs and words the music model chose stay. On a song with two planted mishearings Gemma 4 12B and Qwen3.5 9B both found them (*rowed* -> *road*, *ready to* -> *A detour*); Qwen3.5 9B also changed a sung *I* into *If* - how good it is depends on the writer model, and the record keeps what was heard. The corrections go into the release record's sung lines and text - each kept with the word heard (`timing[].sung.corrected`) - and into `(sung).srt`; the player lists them on the song's page. Nothing runs (no writer model loads) for a song without sung lyrics or without such words, and a record is checked once (`core.sung_check`; Compose Sung Check, Apply Sung Check; a lock keeps it from crossing the sheet music PDF's update of the same record).
- **A login for the Plenio Player** (the same likes, playlists and plays on every device). The player opens with *Who is listening?*: a **user** (picture, then password) keeps their own playlists, favourites, plays, tags and settings on the player's computer - the same on every device that opens the player, also by the network link; the **admin** logs in only on the player's own computer, has the player's state as it was and sets up the users in *Settings > Accounts* (name, password, picture, new password, rename, remove); a **guest** keeps everything in that browser, as before. A device stays logged in and a guest's choice is remembered, so the page asks once; the name and picture in the sidebar log out. Passwords are kept only as PBKDF2-SHA256 hashes (`users.json` beside the state, each user's things in `users/<id>/`), sessions as hashes of their random tokens (an HttpOnly cookie); eight wrong passwords from one device in ten minutes make it wait (`plenio.player.accounts`).
- **Play the MP3**: *Settings > Sound* - a song exported as FLAC (or WAV) and MP3 plays its MP3, about a sixth of the data over the network.
- **HQ Mastering & Cover Art**: the switch at the top of every template is now two cards with an icon - **HQ Mastering** and **Cover Art** -, wide enough for what they say; a click switches one, in the graph and in App mode. The Cover Art card bypasses the template's three cover nodes (no image model loads) and shows a cover bypassed by hand too.

- **Titles from the song, and more of them** (the same titles came again and again). The writer now proposes ten truly different titles after it has written the lyrics - from (a) the lyrics: their theme, central image, a striking phrase, an object, a place, a feeling; (b) a title or theme the brief gives; (c) the genre, mood, voice and tempo -, avoiding the most obvious one and the words 1 577 existing titles had most often (*neon*, *midnight*, *echoes*, *pulse*, *fading* ...). Plenio picks one at random (seeded by the answer, so the same answer gives the same title) and passes over titles a song in `output/plenio` has already, or this ComfyUI picked before (read in the background). Tried with Gemma 4 12B and Qwen3.5 9B: ten different titles each, the layout kept.
- **Cover prompts from the song**: the writer reads (a) the lyrics - what they are about, their central motif, the feelings that could be shown as something seen, the objects and places they name -, then (b) the title and (c) the genre, mood, voice, tempo and language, chooses one strong visual idea and writes 50 to 90 words in an image model's order: the subject, the setting and time of day, the composition for a square, the light, two to four colours, the medium and style that suit the genre, the texture and atmosphere - no text, letters or logos. A cover with the original lyrics gets those lyrics for its picture (until now its writer never saw them).
- **Twelve more classic visuals and thirty more Plenio Drop visuals** (hypnotic and rhythmic, unusual, or very harmonious - MilkDrop's best, made anew). Classics: Digital Rain, LED Wall, VU Meters, Spectrogram, Spirograph, Lava Lamp, Wave Tunnel, Fireworks, Honeycomb, Vinyl (a turning record that writes the music into its grooves), Harmonograph, Spiral Galaxy. Drop: thirty presets after the classics of the MilkDrop preset packs (Geiss, Rovastar, Flexi, Martin, Aderrasi, Krash, Zylot, Eo.S., Unchained ...) rebuilt in Plenio Drop's own format - Cosmic Dust, Twin Spiral, Fractal Garden, Altar of Madness, Mind Blob, Infinite Mosaic, Tokamak, Hypno Disc, Strobe Lattice, Mandala Breath, Harmonic Rose, Cover Droste and more -, each with the music styles it suits for *auto*. Plenio Drop has six new warp patterns for them (`twist`, `fold`, `lens`, `tiles`, `spiral`, `drain`) and MilkDrop's `darkenCenter`; *Drop · Random* uses them too. Settings saved before get the new visuals in their pool once.

### Fixed

- **A click on the Plenio Player button waits only for its own player**: the launcher could wait for a player it had started for another state folder, or one quit a moment before; it now waits only for one of the same state and ports, and starts its own when the one it waited for has stopped.
- **The Plenio Player starts every time** (with well over a thousand songs it sometimes said it could not start, and a second or third click brought it up). The player read all its records before it took its port, so ComfyUI's button - waiting 25 seconds - did not find it, said so, and a further click started a second player that found the port taken (seen in a player.log). The player now answers at once and reads its songs in the background (the page shows *Looking for your songs …* meanwhile): with 2 720 songs and an empty cache it answered after 0.6 s, its library came 9.5 s later. ComfyUI starts one player at a time - a click while one starts waits for it -, a port a busy player holds no longer counts as free, and the button waits up to a minute. Saving the state tries again when Windows holds the file for a moment.
- **The notation cursor follows the music, through the instrumental parts too** (the cursor raced through an instrumental part, then waited at the lyrics). The cursor ran straight from one measured syllable to the next; between syllables more than 4 s apart it ran more than 30 % off the score's tempo in 85 % of the time (on recent covers) - and a word heard in the intro, or a line the take did not sing, pulled it far ahead. The player now finds the score's path through the recording frame by frame (dynamic time warping over the pitch classes of the score's notes and of the music, near the measured warp; `plenio.player.align.follow`, kept in the player's cache): where the take holds, where it leaves part of its plan out. Measured on 20 songs against their vocal stems: the score's vocal notes within 0.15 s of a sung onset 75 % (the syllables: 69 %), their time on the voice 99 % (94 %); a 5 s cut out of an instrumental is found. Songs exported before 1.0.0 follow it too.
- **A style draft no longer stops a run that goes through** (about half of a cover series failed with *The style contains timing or duration*). The check took *warm, driving second half* - a writer's description of the song's build - for a length; a unit counts as a length only after a number now (*3 minutes*, *a 30-second intro*, *2:45*). And a **drafted** style with an error its engine fixes by itself goes on corrected, with a warning that names what was removed (timing, ABC lines, bracketed tags, descriptors past 120 words; for instrumentals vocals and languages) - the user's own style is never changed. Over 1 081 drafted styles, one error remains (193 words), and it is cut.
- **A writer's answer without headings and without lyrics is read** (a cover with its original lyrics: Qwen3.8 27B answered with the title, the style, *none* and the artwork, and the run stopped with *no style block*).
- **The lyrics follow the song again** (the following of the lyrics, in the sheet and in the lyrics, had got worse - often far from what was sung). Since 1.1.0 the lyrics ASR heard a song in one piece of up to four minutes, and over a song with its music Qwen3-ForcedAligner loses its place: a word stretched over the first 24 seconds, a third of the words of zero length, the rest crowded together - on songs of 1.1.0 four in ten syllables lay more than 2 s from their place in the score (1.0: 2 %), lines up to a minute early. Qwen3 now hears a song in pieces of at most 20 s, cut at quiet moments and each heard with 1.5 s of its neighbours; where two pieces heard the same words at a cut, they are paired by their text and kept once (`workers.asr.owned_words`). Measured on 15 YuE2 songs against faster-whisper, an independent timing: 6 % of the words more than 3 s from Whisper's (one piece: 29 %), 72 % of the word starts within 0.15 s of an onset of the separated vocals (59 %). Results heard in one piece are not used again (ASR cache version 2): export a song again to measure it anew.
- **Chinese and Japanese lyrics are matched to what was heard** (songs in Chinese and Japanese: almost none of their words had times). Their lines have no spaces, so a whole phrase was one word; every Kana and Han character is now a token - in the lyrics and in what was heard.
- **A YuE2 plan with a line of other measures no longer stops the run** (a batch: one song of 44 ended in Score Tools with *group 4, Ins: expected 1–4 measures after expanding Z rests* - YuE2 had planned `Z3` for `Z2`, five measures against four): the plan is repaired before it is prepared - surplus whole-bar rests of the longer voice go, the shorter voice is filled with rests where notes stand over, a line of more than four measures becomes several (`native.repair_groups`); no note is lost, the changes are listed in Score Tools' summary.
